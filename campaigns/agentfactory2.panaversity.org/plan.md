# Campaign Plan: The AI Agent Factory Book Launch to the Waitlist

Voice and claim rules: follow `brand/voice.md` for every email in this plan.

## 1. Campaign overview

- **Name:** Start with Horizon 1
- **Summary:** A 4-week, email-only campaign that invites our waitlist to start reading *The AI Agent Factory*, beginning with the intro page.
- **Primary objective:** **300 waitlist members start reading the book** between Mon 7 Dec 2026 and Sun 3 Jan 2027. That is 15% of about 2,000 people. 300 is a single goal with no separate lower target.
- **Secondary objectives:**
  - Learn which messages make readers click, so later campaigns start from evidence.
  - Keep unsubscribes and spam complaints low so the list stays healthy.

**Definition of "start reading":** a unique waitlist member who clicks from one of our emails and opens either *How to Use This Book* (`/how-to-use-this-book`) or *Preface* (`/preface`), the pages after the intro. Each person counts once. The book is free to read on the web, so a start does not involve a purchase or subscription. See section 7 for tracking, which needs a decision from you.

**About the book (from `https://agentfactory2.panaversity.org/llms.txt`):** a web book published chapter by chapter as each is approved. It has front matter, Part 0 (how AI works), Part I (the AI Worker paradigm), Part II (managing AI Workers) and Part III (building AI Workers). Each chapter has lessons, a lab and a "Check yourself" quiz. Readers pick one of three routes in *How to Use This Book*.

## 2. Target audience

- **Primary:** about 2,000 people who joined the waitlist. They asked to hear about the book, so they already know it exists. They are in the **consideration to decision** stage.
- **Who they are:** people who use AI in their daily workflows, from beginners to heavy users. We have no job-title or industry data in this studio, so we do not segment by role.
- **Segments by reading route (self-selected by click):** the book offers three routes, so we let readers choose rather than guess.
  - **New to AI:** start with Part 0, *How AI Works*.
  - **People who delegate work to AI:** start with Part II, *Managing AI Workers*.
  - **People who build with AI:** start with Part III, *Building AI Workers*.
  - Anyone who clicks a route link in E3 joins that segment for E4 to E7.
- **Pain points we can speak to** (from the book's own framing): working with AI by prompting alone, unclear who is in charge of an AI's work, and uncertainty about what comes next.
- **Where they are:** their inbox is the only channel this plan uses.
- **Secondary audience:** none for this campaign.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:** The book is ready to read. Start with Horizon 1, where you assign, govern, and manage an AI Worker.

**Supporting messages:**
1. **Clear:** The book explains how people's work with AI is moving from prompting toward assigning an AI a role.
2. **Practical:** Horizon 1 is where we are now. Learning to manage one AI Worker well is the base for everything after it.
3. **Honest:** Later horizons are forecasts, and the book says so. Humans set purpose, define authority, and stay accountable.
4. **Easy to begin:** It is free to read on the web. One short read is enough to start, and the intro page is the way in.
5. **Honest about release:** Chapters are published as each one is approved, so we say what is available now and never imply the whole book is live.

**Proof points:** only what the book itself says, with a link to the intro page. We have no reviews, reader numbers, or endorsements, so we use none (voice guide, claim 7).

**Message hierarchy per email:** why care, what the book offers, why start now with Horizon 1 (without pressure), one link.

## 7. Success metrics

**Primary KPI:** **300 unique waitlist members start reading** by 3 Jan 2027.

**How it is tracked:** this still needs your input. The book site's analytics are unknown to me. The best method is a unique link per recipient, so a click maps to one person. Because the book is a web resource, a start is the unique visit to `/how-to-use-this-book` or `/preface` from our link. If the site can only count page visits, we use a campaign-tagged link and count unique visitors, which will overstate readers. Please confirm what the site can measure.

**Added secondary KPI:** clicks per reading route (Part 0, Part II, Part III) from E3, to learn which audience responds.

**The math behind 300:**
- 300 ÷ 2,000 = **15% of the list**.
- Each email must keep adding new readers. If most readers start after E1 and E2, the later emails mainly catch the rest.
- I have no history for this list, so I cannot say how likely 15% is. After E1 and E2, check progress: if the count is far below the pace needed, we adjust the later emails rather than push harder.

**Secondary KPIs:**
1. **Click-through rate per email** (unique clicks ÷ delivered).
2. **Click-to-read rate** (readers ÷ unique clickers), which shows whether the landing page works.
3. **Unsubscribe rate per email.** Review any email above 0.5%.
4. **Spam complaint rate.** Stop and review above 0.1%.
5. **Delivery and bounce rate.**

Open rate is shown for information only. Mail apps that preload images make opens unreliable.

**Reporting:** a short check after E1 and E2 (Thu 10 Dec), then weekly each Monday, and a final report on Mon 4 Jan 2027.

## 9. Content guardrails from the book's topics

The book covers certifications, income routes and several AI vendors. These are real parts of the book, but they brush against our never-claim list. Rules for every email:

- **Income and careers (claims 1 and 2):** *From Skills to Income* and *Certification and Portfolio Roadmaps* may be named as chapters. Never say the book leads to a job, income, a certification, or passing an exam. Say "the book covers" or "the book explains".
- **Vendors (claim 5):** we may say the book compares more than one AI vendor and open-source tools. We never say one is better, or that a tool works for the reader in any specific way.
- **Zia Tutor AI and labs:** describe only what the book states. Do not promise outcomes from using the tutor or finishing a lab.
- **Release status (claims 3 and 6):** say chapters are published as approved. Never imply a chapter is live without checking its URL first. No "complete book" wording.
- **Check before every send:** open each linked URL and confirm it loads. The URLs in this plan come from the book's `llms.txt` index.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

1. **15% may be too high for the list.** Mitigation: review after E1 and E2, report plainly if we are behind, and do not use pressure tactics to catch up.
2. **The holidays lower attention.** The window includes Christmas and New Year. Mitigation: keep E5 short, skip holiday days, and let E6 and E7 re-engage.
3. **Weak tracking leads to a wrong count.** Mitigation: test unique links and the first-page check before E1.
4. **Real people must not be emailed during testing.** The studio rule is never to send to real people. Mitigation: test only with made-up addresses in `leads/`. The real send is done by you in your own email platform.
5. **Waitlist consent is unknown.** Mitigation: confirm the list opted in to hear about the book and that every email has a working unsubscribe.
6. **A linked chapter may not be published yet.** The book releases chapter by chapter. Mitigation: link only to pages confirmed live, and recheck on the day of each send.
